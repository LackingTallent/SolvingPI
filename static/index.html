Solving PI

Created for the EVE Online community by Admiral Leyton aka Lacking Tallent · aka Ryan Allen

Enter your characters, your planets, and their scanned resource values — the planner works out the most profitable thing you can actually build with what you've got, using Jita market prices — fetched live where this build can, entered by you where it can't — and the game's real physical limits. Works for any solar system, from 1 to 50 characters.

New here? Read this first (60 seconds)

What this does: you tell it what planets you can reach and how many characters you have; it tells you what to build, how to split your colonies between extraction and factories, and what you'd actually clear per week after tax, shipping and customs. Every answer says how good it is: small operations get a provably optimal plan; big ones get a measured "within X% of the best possible" bound.

  1. Section 1 — Your Operation. Each character individually: planets (max 6 with Interplanetary Consolidation V) and skills — plus one extraction program length for the whole operation. Short programs yield much more per week but need more logins.
  2. Section 2 — Your Systems & Planets. Add planets and enter the survey values from your in-game scan — the raw number, with the familiar percentage shown alongside. Or drop in planet screenshots and let the reader fill them in.
  3. Section 3 — Costs & Market. Taxes, fees, freight, and Jita prices (fetch what's available live, type in the rest) — including whether you sell instantly or list orders, which changes real money.
  4. Sections 4-5 — Goal & Results. Pick a goal (max profit, a quota, a login budget, or compare everything), hit Solve, and get per-character build sheets plus the analytics: what's bottlenecking you, what the next character or skill is worth, and whether each input is better extracted, refined, or bought.

Numbers here come from CCP's published formulas and the game's own data, verified — where something can't be known (like another player's POCO tax), you set it and the tool says what it assumed. It never silently guesses.

What this step decides: what your planets can physically produce.

Raw survey values, not just percentages

The engine works in the game's own unit: the per-cycle extraction value your survey window shows (what ESI calls qty_per_cycle). The familiar percentage is shown alongside as a translation — 100% is the value where one extractor roughly feeds 8 basic industry facilities on 6-hour programs.

The physical cap — corrected from earlier versions

An extraction colony produces

min( what its facilities can process, extractor supply ÷ 150 )

8 basic facilities top out at 53,760 P1/week (20 per 30-minute cycle each). A hot planet above 100% does NOT produce beyond that cap — unless the plan adds more basic facilities, which the tool will propose when the surplus is real. Earlier versions scaled linearly past the cap; this one never claims P1 the buildings cannot make.

Why planet type matters

Each type carries a fixed set of raw resources. A Lava planet has no Aqueous Liquids at any density. The tool refuses plans that need a resource the planet does not have — by name — rather than quietly assuming.

How to use Solving PI

This tool works out what to build on your planets and how much you will make. You enter what you have, it does the arithmetic. Nothing is saved to a server and it never asks for your EVE login.

The short version

  1. Enter your characters — each one individually, with their real skills.
  2. Enter your planets and their scanned resource values (or drop in screenshots).
  3. Enter your costs — tax, fees, shipping — and load or enter Jita prices.
  4. Pick a goal and press Solve.
  5. You get a build sheet per character, plus the analytics that explain it.

Step 1 — Your operation

Every character is modeled individually. One character can run six planets at most, and that needs Interplanetary Consolidation V. If your main runs six and two alts run one each, that is eight colonies, not eighteen — the tool adds up what you tell it. From 1 to 50 characters, and a one-character operation gets the same rigor (in fact small operations get a provably optimal answer, because they can be searched exhaustively).

Extraction program length is how long you set your extractors to run. Short programs give more per week but need more logins. Six hours is full pace; a day gives about 81% of it; a week about 34%. The Results section can show this trade as ISK per week and ISK per login, so you can pick what fits your life.

Step 2 — Your systems & planets

Resource values come from your in-game planet scan. Enter the raw per-cycle value the survey shows; the familiar percentage appears alongside. Values above 100% are real and the tool never caps them — but it also never claims output your buildings cannot physically process. When a hot planet outruns its factories, the tool says so and proposes a bigger layout instead of inventing units.

The screenshot reader fills planets in from survey screenshots, entirely in your browser. Check its work — it is a convenience, not a data source.

Step 3 — Costs & market

  • Customs tax — what your POCO or skyhook charges. The game taxes fixed base values per tier, not market price — the tool gets this right.
  • Sales tax and broker fee — what the market takes when you sell.
  • Price basis — instant hits existing orders (no broker fee, worse price); patient lists orders (broker fee, better price, if they fill). This is real money and the tool prices both honestly.
  • Shipping — ISK per cubic metre, charged on real cargo volume.

Sourcing decides where each input comes from: extract it, refine it from bought ore (150 units of ore per unit made — roughly 1.9× the customs and 4× the freight of buying finished), or buy it finished. The analytics re-solve all three per input and tell you what your current choice is leaving on the table.

Steps 4-5 — Goal & results

Four ways to define “best”: most output of your chosen product (then priced honestly — a loss shows as a loss), a quota (minimum colonies to hit a weekly amount), a login budget (best net within your sessions per week), or compare everything (the profit ranking across every product your operation could make).

Every result names its bottleneck (add capacity there first), its answer quality (exact, or within a measured percentage of the best possible), and reconciles every ISK to one ledger you can open and read line by line. A plan that loses money says so with a minus sign — losses are never hidden.

The reference sections

  • Market Reference — live Jita prices for all 101 items with trends.
  • Price History — the same data charted, plus live order-book depth.
  • PI Templates — ready-made colony layouts. Copy, paste in game, done.
  • System Status — whether EVE and its API are up.

Saving your work

The tool autosaves to your browser as you type. Save My Data downloads a file; use it to move between computers or keep a backup.

Things worth knowing

  • It will not click the buttons for you. It works out what to build and where. You still build it.
  • Nothing touches your account. No login, no password. Only public data.
  • Screenshots stay on your computer. The image reading happens in your browser.
  • Prices move. Recalculate before committing to something large.
